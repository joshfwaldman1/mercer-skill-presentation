2. How I explain Mercer now

The hearing is a tool. The goal is a record he can work with.

Mercer is an OB-GYN at a county hospital. He got probation on Dec 11, 2025. He was suspended as an “imminent danger” on Dec 16. No patient was named. The Board terminated his privileges on Jan 21, 2026.

The MEC only asked the Board to keep the suspension. The Board went further, after a 14-minute closed session.

The only outside reviewer rated Case A “standard of care met” and Case B “met with some concerns.”

His weak spot is the H&P. He documented a patient he may not have seen, and a bill went out. He admitted it at the Nov 20 meeting.

What he needs is an NPDB record and an OPMC outcome that let him work again. Winning the hearing matters only if it gets him there.

Sources: R1/03 Plan of Correction p.3, R1/03 2025-12-16 MEC Letter, R2/MEC Letter p.1, R2/Board Minutes p.1, R2/Summary p.5, R2/Notes pp.1-2.

4. Claude’s contribution

Testing caught a plan built on an unchecked premise

One test run recommended negotiating the NPDB report away. It flagged that premise as unverified and recommended the plan anyway.

We checked the NPDB Guidebook. A report is voided only when the review body vacates the suspension. Resigning while under investigation is itself reportable.

So the ask changed. Get the suspension vacated. Never resign first.

Checked at npdb.hrsa.gov/guidebook/EClinicalPrivileges.jsp on Oct 16, 2026. All 19 legal checks are in LEGAL_VERIFICATION.md.

There are no Mercer facts in the skill. The case lives in three working files the student saves and brings back. The same skill works on the next case.

The citation rule

Every factual sentence says where it comes from.

  1. A record cite. The file and the page, for example R2/Summary p.5.
  2. A legal cite. The rule itself, for example Bylaws VIII §5.i p.31 or CPLR 217(1).
  3. A status label when there is no cite. VERIFIED means I checked it at the cite. INFERRED means my reasoning, stated in the line. OPEN means the file doesn’t establish it, and the line says what would. [M] means Mercer’s word alone.

Never invent a page number. If you can’t find the source, it is OPEN.

The separate citation-check skill runs this rule as a script. My Oct 14 notebook passes with 0 uncited lines. The script checks that a cite exists. It does not check that the cite is right, and it skips table rows. Those still need a person.

6. Demonstration

What a new user sees

  1. It lists the files it read, the ones it only saw, and the scans.
  2. It asks what you think before it shows you anything.
  3. It builds the case map in pieces and makes you explain it back.
  4. It asks which evidence tells the two sides apart.
  5. It recommends a plan, then asks what would change your mind.
  6. It gives you three files to bring back next time: the case overview, the evidence-and-issues map, and the strategy with a change log.

To run it, upload the zip under Customize, then Skills, in claude.ai. Start a Project with the case files. Ask for the skill by name.

7. Adaptation

What happened when I changed a key premise

HYPOTHETICAL TEST

The test said the hearing request went by plain email to Mara Whitcomb on Feb 24, with no receipt. That is not a case fact.

The scripted student answer was that this would change the date math. The skill said it changes much more.

  • Three defects, not one. Wrong person, because Whitcomb is not the CEO. Wrong method, because there is no receipt. Maybe late, because the email deadline was Feb 20.
  • A possible rescue. If the certified letter arrived on Jan 26 or later, Feb 24 was on time.
  • A new problem. Feb 24 is the day after the exit offer lapsed. The hospital will say he asked only once the deal died.
  • The plan changed. The hearing becomes something to win back. The May 21 Article 78 date becomes a real deadline. The first step becomes a corrected request to the Acting CEO by certified mail.

It kept the hypothetical in separate files and left the original record-based version alone.

8. Limits

What I still don’t know

  • What happened on Oct 31. Only Mercer and the billing records can say.
  • Whether the hearing request was on time and delivered the right way.
  • What the Board saw in its closed session on Jan 21.
  • Whether a negotiated vacatur will hold up with the NPDB.
  • What Mercer wants. He might want to clear his name, or he might want to work again soon.
  • The 27 peer-review packets are not in the file. The case cites still need Westlaw.

Each of these could change the plan. The plan says how. A lawyer still has to make the call on each one.
