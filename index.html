What theory do we take into the hearing?

I started with pretext. My case-strategy skill questioned it, one question at a time. I ended somewhere else.

Where I started

Pretext

Leadership decided to get rid of Mercer. Then they built the clinical case to justify it.

What the skill did

Five questions

It asked what the theory needs, what cuts against it, and what the hearing actually requires.

Where I landed

Overreaction

The problems were real and already handled on Dec 11. Firing him went beyond the evidence.

Where I started

The pretext theory

On Oct 14 my notebook told it this way. Four days after telling Mercer “we want you to be successful,” leadership fired him from his job, called him an imminent danger without naming a patient, and had its own department chair find cases after the fact.

The plan was to go into the hearing and win on that story.

My Oct 14 notebook, 05_Theory_of_the_Case. R1/03 Plan of Correction p.3, R2/Venn Letter p.1.

How the skill questioned it

Five questions, and what the record answered

These are the questions the claims-counterclaims skill asks.

Skill asks

What evidence shows a motive?

Record says

Almost none. There is a call-pay dispute and some new OB nursing positions. No document shows anyone wanted him gone for another reason.

Pretext needs a motive. We don’t have one.

Notebook 04, O1 and O9. R1/06 HR Minutes Jan 12.

Skill asks

What is the most dangerous fact against your theory?

Record says

His own words at the Nov 20 meeting: “Did see her.” Then: “Forgot that I did not see the patient.” And a bill went out.

A pretext story says the case was invented. He admitted part of it.

R2/Notes pp.1-2.

Skill asks

What does the hearing actually make you prove?

Record says

Mercer must show the action “lacks substantial basis” or is “arbitrary, unreasonable, or capricious.”

We don’t have to prove motive at all. Pretext takes on a burden the rule doesn’t require.

Bylaws VIII §5.i p.31.

Skill asks

Does the outside review prove your theory?

Record says

It helps, but it doesn’t decide it. The reviewer saw two charts. One was “met with some concerns.” It said nothing about record keeping, which is half the Board’s stated grounds.

Both sides can live with it. It doesn’t tell the theories apart.

R2/Summary p.5. R1/05 Board Letter p.1.

Skill asks

Which fact would the theories predict differently?

Record says

Timing. If care concerns came up before Dec 12, the “built after the fact” story fails. One packet says an issue was raised with him “before.” When is open.

Even the timing is shaky. Pretext rests on it alone.

R2/Summary p.4. Notebook 02, row 19.

Where I landed

The timing evidence doesn’t go away. It moves. It stops being proof of a secret motive and becomes proof the decision was arbitrary.

The hearing plan

What we argue

  1. There is no substantial basis.The outside reviewer found the standard met. No packet gives an overall rating. Only 3 of 27 call anything a deviation. 5 packets have missing or mismatched exhibits. R2/Summary pp.4-5
  2. The decision was arbitrary.The chart review was ordered the same day as the job termination notice. Every write-up came after the suspension. One is dated after the firing. The Board went past the MEC after a 14-minute closed session. R2/Venn p.1, R2/Summary pp.2-4, R2/Board Minutes
  3. The procedure broke.The suspension should have lapsed after 14 days. The MEC proposed nothing until Jan 20. The hearing is overdue. Bylaws VII §2.d, VIII §3.a
  4. The panel has to be clean.Venn, Doran, Calder, Keene and Whitcomb all took part. They can’t sit. The objection is due 10 days before the hearing. Bylaws VIII §4.a, §4.c
  5. Hold them to their stated grounds.The Board fired him for care and records. The nurses’ conduct complaint isn’t a stated ground. R1/05 Board Letter p.1
The worst fact

Don’t fight the H&P. Frame it. He admitted it on the spot. The hospital chose correction on Dec 11. The Jan 21 grounds don’t mention fraud. It is a documentation lapse already punished. It is not proof of imminent danger.

One catch. His lawyer can’t question witnesses at this hearing, so Mercer testifies himself, and what he says can reach the state licensing board. Interview him about Oct 31 first. Bylaws VIII §5.e

What would change this

Two facts could flip it

If Mercer never saw the patient and the bill was never refunded, the H&P becomes fraud exposure. Then a quiet negotiated exit beats a hearing.

If the hearing request was late, the hearing may be gone. Then the fight moves to court and to negotiation.

What the checks found

What the tools caught

citation-check, on my working documents

Rules and inferences stated as facts

“File the protective Article 78 by 5/21” had no rule behind it. Fix: CPLR 217(1), four months from Jan 21.

“The 4/23 notice of claim” had no rule behind it. Fix: GML §50-e.

“If he never examined her, it’s fraud exposure” read as fact. It is my inference. Fix: label it INFERRED and cite R2/Notes p.1.

My Oct 14 notebook: 0 flags. Today’s working documents: 17 flags, 6 of them real.

Testing the tutor

Testing caught a premise nobody checked

One plan assumed we could negotiate the NPDB report away.

The NPDB Guidebook says only a vacated suspension voids the report. Resigning first is itself reportable.

The ask changed. Vacate the suspension. Never resign first.

citation-check couldn’t catch this. The line was labeled “research needed,” and a labeled line passes.
